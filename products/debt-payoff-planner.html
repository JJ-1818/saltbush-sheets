About this template

Juggling a credit card, a buy now pay later plan and a car loan makes it hard to know where any extra money should go. This planner answers that with your own numbers.

Enter up to 6 debts with their balance, yearly interest rate and minimum payment, plus how much extra you can pay each month. The workbook runs two complete month-by-month plans on the same budget: Snowball (smallest balance first) and Avalanche (highest interest rate first).

The Dashboard puts them side by side: months until debt-free, your debt-free month, total interest and when your first debt is cleared. The Debts tab shows the month each debt is cleared under each method, and the Payment Log tracks what you've paid and your latest balances so you can see your progress.

Comes with example data so you can see how it works first. Works in Excel and the free Google Sheets app. A planning tool, not financial advice: interest is estimated monthly and minimums are treated as fixed.

Questions

Will it tell me which method to use?

It shows both on your numbers (months, debt-free month and total interest) so you can choose. You pick the method on the Setup tab.

What format is it?

An instant-download Excel workbook (.xlsx) that also opens in Google Sheets. Prices are in AUD.

What's the difference between Snowball and Avalanche?

Snowball pays off the smallest balance first for quick wins. Avalanche pays off the highest interest rate first, which usually costs less interest. The planner calculates both on your numbers so you can choose.

How many debts can I add?

Up to 6. If you have more, you can combine small ones with similar rates into one line.

Does it work with buy now pay later?

Yes. Enter 0% as the rate and your monthly instalment as the minimum payment.

Is the interest exact?

It's an estimate: the yearly rate divided by 12, added monthly, with fixed minimums. Lenders calculate slightly differently, so use your statements for exact balances. It's a planning tool, not financial advice.

Does it connect to my bank?

No. You type in your balances and payments, which keeps your bank login private.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Open it in the free Google Sheets app.
