About this template

If rate-rise talk has you wondering what +0.25% or +1% would mean for your household, a repayment figure is only half the answer. You also need to know what it does to your budget.

Enter your income, up to 5 loans, your rent if you pay it, and your monthly costs. The workbook tests five scenarios you can change, starting at +0.25%, +0.50%, +0.75%, +1% and +2%. It shows your extra cost per month, fortnight and week, what's left each month, and an OK, Tight or Short verdict.

It also finds the biggest rate rise your budget can carry, with and without the trims you list, and how many months your savings would last in a shortfall. First home savers can enter a loan they are considering, and renters can test their own rent increases.

The Ladder sheet shows repayments and what's left at every step up to +3%. Example data is included so you can see how it works before entering your own numbers. This is a general budgeting tool, not financial advice, and it does not predict rates.

Questions

What format is it?

It's an instant digital download: an Excel workbook (.xlsx) that also opens in Google Sheets. Prices are in AUD.

Do I need to share my bank details?

No. You type in your own numbers, and nothing connects to your bank.

Does it predict what the RBA will do?

No. It shows what different rate rises would do to your numbers. You choose the scenarios, and you can change them at any time.

I rent. Is it still useful?

Yes. Choose Rent only on Setup, enter your weekly rent and set a rent rise for each scenario. The sheet shows the extra cost and what's left each month.

I'm saving for my first home. How do I use it?

Enter the loan you are considering on the Loans sheet, then add your income and expected costs. You can see how that loan would fit your budget at each scenario.

Can it handle fixed loans and splits?

Yes. Enter each split as its own loan, up to 5 in total. Set 'Rate can change?' to No to hold a fixed rate, or Yes to test what happens when the fixed period ends.

Will the repayments match my bank's figures exactly?

They are estimates based on monthly compounding and the details you enter, so your lender's figures may differ slightly. Fortnightly and weekly amounts are monthly equivalents.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a general budgeting tool. Check your numbers with your lender or a licensed adviser before making a decision.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Open it in the free Google Sheets app.
